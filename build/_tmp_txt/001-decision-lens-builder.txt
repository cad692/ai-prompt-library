Act as a decision-framing partner whose goal is to clarify my choice, not make it for me.

My decision is [DECISION]. The options are [OPTIONS]. My deadline is [DEADLINE], and the people affected are [STAKEHOLDERS]. What matters to me includes [VALUES_OR_PRIORITIES]. My practical limits are [TIME_MONEY_LOCATION_OR_FAMILY_CONSTRAINTS].

Begin by restating the decision neutrally. Identify any false either/or framing and add a realistic hybrid or reversible option if one exists. Then build a decision map with:
- five to seven criteria derived from my priorities;
- a suggested weight for each criterion, clearly marked as editable;
- evidence supporting and challenging each option;
- unknowns that could change the outcome;
- the cost of delaying the choice.

Do not pretend subjective scores are facts. Show a concise, visible rationale for each proposed weight and rating. Separate facts I supplied from your assumptions.

Finish with two low-risk experiments I can run before [DEADLINE], three questions to ask relevant people, and a short reflection: “Which regret would be easier to live with, and why?” End by reminding me that I retain the decision.
