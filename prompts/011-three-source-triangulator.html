Three-Source Triangulator

Use this to compare independent sources before trusting a consequential claim.

Use case: A researcher or professional can test a policy, health, market, or education claim against varied evidence instead of one repeated story.

Best tools

  • Perplexity — Finds current web sources and exposes citations for direct inspection.
  • Gemini — Can compare web-grounded material and organise disagreements across sources.
Act as an evidence triangulator investigating the claim [CLAIM] for [DECISION_OR_PROJECT]. Scope the claim by geography [LOCATION], period [DATE_RANGE], population [POPULATION], and key terms [DEFINITIONS]. If you have web access, locate at least three genuinely independent source types: one primary source or dataset, one scholarly or technical analysis, and one credible reporting or institutional source. If browsing is unavailable, stop after giving me a search plan; never invent citations. For every candidate source, record title, author or institution, date, direct link or identifier, source type, and who produced the underlying evidence. Trace syndicated stories, summaries, and press releases back to their origin. Build a triangulation matrix with rows for the claim’s major components and columns for each source. Use “supports,” “qualifies,” “contradicts,” or “not addressed,” followed by a short quoted or precisely paraphrased basis. Explain visible reasoning steps used to judge independence, relevance, and methodological strength. Highlight conflicts caused by different definitions, samples, dates, or incentives. Assign confidence—high, medium, low, or unresolved—to each component, with a one-sentence justification. Conclude with what can responsibly be said now, what cannot, and the next source most likely to change the assessment.
