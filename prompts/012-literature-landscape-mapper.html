Literature Landscape Mapper

Use this to map a field's themes, methods, debates, and landmark studies.

Use case: A student beginning a dissertation or professional review can understand how a topic is organised before reading narrowly.

Best tools

  • Perplexity — Supports discovery of scholarly and institutional sources with inspectable links.
  • NotebookLM — Synthesizes themes across a user-provided collection while grounding answers in those sources.

Fill in your details

We ask only for the parts in square brackets. Your answers are inserted into the prompt below.

Your filled prompt (preview)

Map the research landscape around [TOPIC] so I can plan an ethical, focused literature review. Boundaries: [DISCIPLINE], [GEOGRAPHY], [YEARS], [POPULATION], and [LANGUAGES]. My purpose is [COURSE_DISSERTATION_POLICY_OR_WORK_PROJECT]. Use only sources I provide in [SOURCE_LIST] or sources you can actually retrieve and cite. Mark any item known only from metadata; do not imply that you read a full paper when you did not. Organise the field as a map, not a chronological essay: - major conceptual clusters and how they connect; - recurring research methods and data sources; - landmark or frequently cited works; - active disagreements; - underrepresented settings, especially Pakistan or comparable contexts where relevant. For each cluster, name two to four representative sources with stable links, DOI, or complete citation details. Add a one-sentence reason each source belongs there. Distinguish empirical findings, theoretical proposals, and reviews. Then draw a text-based pathway for reading: five foundation items, three contrasting items, and two recent items. Explain the selection criteria. End with six database search strings using synonyms and Boolean operators, plus inclusion and exclusion rules I can apply consistently. Remind me to verify every citation and read original sources before making academic claims.
