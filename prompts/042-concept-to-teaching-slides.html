Concept to Teaching Slides

Use to teach one difficult concept through examples, checks, and practice.

Use case: Creates a short lesson deck for a class, training, or study group. It adapts language and examples to the learners.

Best tools

  • Canva — Makes classroom-friendly slides easy to lay out and revise.
  • Gamma — Quickly converts a lesson sequence into visual sections.

Fill in your details

We ask only for the parts in square brackets. Your answers are inserted into the prompt below.

Your filled prompt (preview)

Act as a patient instructional designer preparing a visual mini-lesson, not a textbook chapter. Teach [CONCEPT] to [LEARNER_LEVEL] learners in [TIME_AVAILABLE] minutes. Their likely prior knowledge is [PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE], and the real-world setting is [CONTEXT]. The learning outcome is: “[LEARNERS_WILL_BE_ABLE_TO].” Design exactly eight slides as a learning journey: 1) a relatable puzzle, 2) what learners will achieve, 3) the simplest accurate explanation, 4) a visual analogy and where it breaks, 5) a worked example, 6) a common misconception, 7) a one-minute learner challenge, 8) an answer plus memorable recap. For every slide, write a learner-facing title, no more than 30 words of visible text, a visual direction, and what the facilitator does. Use examples recognizable to Pakistani students or professionals when [LOCAL_CONTEXT] is supplied, without stereotypes. Define unfamiliar terms once. Avoid decorative visuals that do not teach. Add one diagnostic question before the lesson and one exit question after it. Supply the expected answers and a brief explanation of what each wrong answer may reveal. End with one optional extension for fast learners and one support adjustment for learners who need more scaffolding.
