Constraint-Led Idea Lab

Use this to generate feasible ideas within real limits instead of generic brainstorming.

Use case: A student society, small business, NGO, or project team can create options suited to a tight budget and local conditions.

Best tools

  • Gemini — Generates and reorganizes diverse concepts from detailed contextual constraints.
  • DeepSeek — Offers capable text brainstorming and structured option comparison.

Fill in your details

We ask only for the parts in square brackets. Your answers are inserted into the prompt below.

Run a constraint-led idea lab for the goal [GOAL], prioritising usefulness over sheer quantity. The audience is [AUDIENCE]. We have [BUDGET], [TIME], [TEAM_SIZE], and access to [RESOURCES]. We must respect [NON_NEGOTIABLES], avoid [EXCLUSIONS], and account for [LOCAL_CONTEXT]. Success means [MEASURE_OF_SUCCESS]. Generate ideas in three deliberately different waves: Wave 1 — five dependable ideas using familiar methods. Wave 2 — five combinations that borrow a mechanism from [OTHER_FIELD]. Wave 3 — three “reverse assumption” ideas that challenge how this is normally done. Each idea must fit on three lines: concept, why it fits the constraints, and its weakest point. Do not repeat the same core mechanism under new names. Next, apply a hard filter: eliminate anything that exceeds a non-negotiable limit and explain each rejection in one sentence. Score the survivors from 1–5 for impact, effort, inclusion, and testability. Select a portfolio of three: one safe, one balanced, and one bold. For each finalist, design a [DAYS]-day pilot requiring no more than [PILOT_BUDGET]. End with the single piece of evidence that would make us stop, adapt, or scale each pilot.

Pro tip: Include one unusual constraint; it often produces more distinctive ideas than asking for creativity.
Expected output: A diverse shortlist of feasible concepts with tests and rejection reasons.
