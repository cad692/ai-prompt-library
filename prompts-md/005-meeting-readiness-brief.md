# 005 — Meeting Readiness Brief

**Category:** assistants · **Difficulty:** Intermediate

**Usage:** Use this to enter a meeting with clear outcomes, questions, and fallback positions.

**Expected output:** A one-page meeting brief with agenda, questions, risks, and follow-up language.

**Tip:** Define one minimum acceptable outcome so the meeting remains useful even if time runs short.

## Best tools
- **Claude**: Synthesizes lengthy background notes into a coherent preparation brief.
- **ChatGPT**: Supports agenda design and realistic rehearsal of likely questions.

## Prompt

```text
Act as my meeting strategist and prepare a concise briefing for [MEETING_NAME] on [DATE].

Context: [BACKGROUND]
Participants and roles: [PARTICIPANTS]
My desired outcome: [IDEAL_OUTCOME]
Minimum acceptable outcome: [MINIMUM_OUTCOME]
Sensitive points: [SENSITIVE_POINTS]
Evidence or documents available: [MATERIALS]
Duration: [MINUTES]

Build a one-page readiness brief. Open with a two-sentence purpose statement I can say aloud. Arrange an agenda that fits the stated duration, including explicit minutes for decisions and questions. Add a stakeholder read: what each participant may care about, labelled as either known or inferred.

Create five high-value questions, ordered from exploratory to decision-focused. Anticipate three difficult questions I may receive and draft honest, compact responses that do not fabricate missing data. For each sensitive point, suggest neutral wording and a phrase for redirecting discussion if it becomes unproductive.

Add a mini decision log with columns for decision, owner, deadline, and evidence needed. Finish with a 30-second closing summary and a follow-up email skeleton. If the meeting goal is too broad for [MINUTES], explicitly recommend what to defer.
```
